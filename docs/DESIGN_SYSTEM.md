# TouchLine: design system

The look of the game, written down so every screen is built the same way. The owner's brief: a proper logo, blue instead of green, a nicer design and a cleaner layout; on the Season setup screen "less text, more pictures", keeping the detail and the customisation; and the name written **TouchLine**, not in capitals. The goal is a product that looks finished and friendly on a phone and on a laptop.

Where it lives:

The rule for everyone building screens: **use the tokens and the components below; no raw colours, no new one-off styles.** If a screen needs something this document does not cover, add it here and to `styles.css` first.

## 1. Principles

1. **Night football, calm, confident.** Deep night-navy surfaces, one cyan (a deep blue in Daylight) for actions, selection and focus, and a cyan-to-violet glow on the one primary button. Magenta lives only in the art. Neon is an accent, never a fill for whole panels. Status colours (good, warn, bad, gold) only ever mean status.
2. **Less text, more picture.** Badges, flags, league marks, pitch diagrams and numbers carry the information. Explanations sit behind an info icon, not in paragraphs under controls.
3. **One primary action per region.** Each panel, bar or modal has at most one `.btn.primary`. Everything else is secondary or ghost.
4. **Mixed case.** Titles and buttons are written as sentences. Capitals are kept for small labels and codes only.
5. **Phone first.** Every screen works at 390 px with no sideways scroll, and the main action is reachable with a thumb.

## 2. Name and logo

**The name is TouchLine**: one word, capital T and capital L, everywhere it is displayed (`APP_NAME` in `brand.ts`). Never "TOUCHLINE", "Touch Line" or "Touchline". Lower-case `touchline` stays only where it is a slug: the npm package, the repository, `localStorage` keys (`touchline-save-v1`), download file names and the service worker cache. Engine strings are unchanged: `shareText` keeps its `'Touchline'` default, and the UI passes `APP_NAME`.

**The mark** is the owner's sketch: a football corner flag (a white pole standing on the touchline, its foot ending at the line's top edge with nothing below it, a red pennant flying right from its top), a classic black-and-white ball resting on the line to the right of the pole, and a thin white touchline running on past both, on the blue tile. It replaced the pitch-corner mark of the first redesign pass. Everything is drawn from `brand.ts`, on a 64-unit grid (y down):

| Part | Full tier (64 px and up) |
|---|---|
| Tile | 64 × 64, corner radius 15, linear gradient `#3b82f6` (top left) to `#1d4ed8` (bottom right); no grass, the tile stays pure blue |
| Touchline | x 9 to 57 at y 51, stroke 1.6 (44 % of the first logo's 3.6), round caps |
| Pole | x 16, stroke 2.8, round caps, y 11.2 to 48.8: with its cap it ends exactly on the line's top edge (50.2); drawn as a rounded post (a slim white highlight left of centre and a soft shade along the right edge, one gradient over the white stroke) with a round finial (r 2 at y 10.6) carrying a radial highlight |
| Pennant | from the pole, top 12, bottom 27.2, tip (38.5, 19.2), the tip rounded (r 0.7); both edges carry the same wave (amplitude 1.7); drawn as cloth (see below) at strength 1.3 |
| Ball | r 11.5 at x 41.5, resting on the line's top edge (4 units of line run on past it): a true truncated icosahedron (front pentagon, five hexagons, rim panels), projected with curved seams, each panel shaded by its facing angle, a navy rim shade and a highlight at the top left |

Three detail tiers, picked from the rendered size by `markTier(px)`:

| Tier | Sizes | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| `full` | 64 px and up (app icons 192 and 512, apple-touch, the Home hero) | Everything above |
| `mid` | 22 to 63 px (the top bar, the loading screen, 24 to 48 px icons) | Flat ball: a white disc with a soft grey edge (`#c3cddb`) and black pentagons only, no seams or shading; pole 4 (standing on the line, a plain stroke, no finial), line 2.4 (half the first logo's 4.8), ball r 12 at x 41; the wave drops to 1.1 and the cloth to strength 0.6, so at 32 px it still reads as a clean red pennant |
| `tiny` | under 22 px (`favicon.svg`) | On the pixel grid (4 units are 1 px at 16 px): pole x 16 to 20 and y 8 to 48, standing on the line, which is one pixel (y 48 to 52, x 8 to 60), butt caps; a straight pennant 20 × 20 units (5 px) in flat red `#ef4444` (no folds or shade), ending clear of the ball; the colour ball (r 12 at 40, 36) carries real pentagon panels, clipped to the disc: the front one and those on the lower rim (`tinyPanels`), so the favicon still reads as a football when the browser draws it at 32 device px (one centred patch read as an eye there) while the top of the ball stays a clean white arc against the tile at 16 px (all the rim panels turned it into a grey check); the mono and inverse balls keep a single pentagon nudged up and left |

**The cloth** (`pennantCloth` and `clothStops` in `brand.ts`, colours in `MARK.cloth`). On the waving tiers and the lockup the pennant is one shape (`<path id="…-pc">`, in `<defs>`, animated once) drawn three times with `<use>`:

1. **Folds**: a linear gradient of 17 stops from the pole to the tip, slanted a little (its end 0.3 of the cloth's length below the tip) so the bands lean back from the upper edge. Each stop's light comes from the slope of the same wave the edges carry plus a ripple at twice its frequency travelling with it, so there are two or three soft light and dark bands; the red `#ef4444` is mixed toward `#ff8c7e` where the cloth faces the light and toward `#8c1212` where it turns away, with a soft knee so plain red stays between the crests, and the cloth by the pole is held flat.
2. **Light and shade**: a vertical gradient on the shape's own box (so it follows the cloth as it waves): white at 34 % along the upper edge fading out by 16 %, nothing in the middle, and `#450a0a` rising to 42 % along the lower edge (times the strength).
3. **The pole's shadow**: `#450a0a` at 50 % where the cloth meets the pole, gone 16 % of the way along.

Strength (`MARK.cloth.strength`) scales all three: 1.3 on the full tile, 0.6 on the mid tile, 1.1 on the lockup; the tiny tier is a single flat red path. The tip is rounded (`tip`, a five-point arc inside `tipX`), so it ends in a soft curve rather than a spike.

**The lockup** (`Lockup`) is the open, tile-less version: the mark's drawing at the left (the corner flag standing on a short touchline, the ball resting on it right of the pole, the line ending a little past the ball), then "TouchLine" standing on the same ground: the name's baseline is the line's top edge. There is no line under the name. Lengths are in u = height / 64 (`LOCKUP` in `brand.ts`, mirrored in the `.tl-*` rules of `styles.css`; a test checks the two agree):

The mark is sized to the name (the owner's "balanced and well ratioed with the text"; a test holds the ratios):

- The name is real text in Sora 700 at 48u from 57u (cap height 35.0u, x-height 26.4u, letter spacing 0.012em); its line box starts at 10.7u (Sora's baseline sits 0.84 of the size down a line-height-1 box), so its baseline lands at 51u, on the line. "Touch" (`.tl-touch`) and "Line" each carry a top-lit gradient clipped to the letters over a solid fallback colour: on Floodlit "Touch" `#ffffff` to `#c9d5e8` and "Line" `#9ccaff` to `#4b8df8`; on Daylight "Touch" `#2d3d5e` to `#0d1526` and "Line" `#2563eb` to `#1e3a8a` (every end clears 4.5:1 on the page). Under `forced-colors` both fall back to plain text.
- The mark, finial to baseline, is 40.8u: 1.16 cap heights. The pennant's top (14u) sits 2u above the cap tops; the ball's diameter (24u) is 0.91 of the x-height.
- The line: top 51u, 2u thick (1 px at the smallest lockup, 32 px), from the pole's left edge (7.5u) for 37u, to 4u past the ball; then 12.5u (about half a letter, 0.38 of an "o") before the name.
- Finial top (10.2u) to line bottom (53u) is centred in the 64u box (31.6u).
- The pole: x 9u, 3u wide, 13.4u to 49.5u, its round foot on the line's top edge; a rounded post like the full tile's (highlight and shade over `--tl-ink`) with a finial r 2.2u at 12.4u. The pennant: top 14u, bottom 26u, tip (37.5u, 19u), tip radius 1.3u, cloth at strength 1.1.
- The ball: r 12u resting on the line at x 28.5u, a clear 6u right of the pole and clear of the pennant at every frame of the wave. It is the flat tier below a 40 px diameter (every size in the app today), with a darker edge (`#94a3b8`), plus a soft navy rim shade, a small highlight at the top left and a contact shadow (a soft ellipse, r 0.85 × 0.14 of the ball, drawn under it) where it sits on the line.
- Width is about 317u: 515 px at 104 px high (the Home hero), 297 px at 60 (the hero on a phone), 198 px at 40 (the top bar), 158 px at 32 (the top bar on a phone).

Colours: on Floodlit the pole and the line are white; on Daylight they, and a thin ring round the ball, take `--text` (`#0a1b51`), the same navy as the name. The pennant stays red on both. All of it follows the theme through CSS variables (`--tl-ink`, `--tl-ball-ring`).

**Motion.** CSS keyframes only, no JavaScript; the resting state is the finished logo, so `prefers-reduced-motion` switches the animations off and shows it.

- **Shine:** an `aria-hidden` copy of the name lies over it (generated content, `::before { content: attr(data-text) }`, so the page text, find-in-page and copy see the name once), its text transparent, its background a narrow 110° band (pale blue edge `#93c5fd`, white core on Floodlit; `#bfdbfe` edge, white core on Daylight) clipped to the letters with `background-clip: text`; `background-position` sweeps it left to right once. Alongside it a `mask-image` wipe at the same angle and size holds the letters ahead of the band at 68 % and brings them up to full behind it, so the glint reads on white "Touch" as well as on blue "Line". The shine uses an even ease (`cubic-bezier(.45, .05, .4, 1)`) so it is seen over both halves.
- **Line:** `transform: scaleX(0 → 1)` from the left, so the short touchline draws itself out from under the pole.
- **Ball:** it fades in clear of the pole and rolls 5u along the line to its spot, turning 23.9° (distance / radius, so it rolls rather than slides). It never touches the pole or the letters.
- **Pennant flutter:** the cloth waves in a light wind on every lockup (the Home hero and the top bar), always on a desktop and for three loops (`TOUCH_WAVE_LOOPS` in `Logo.tsx`, about 8 s) on a touch screen (`hover: none`), after which the cloth goes back to its resting shape and folds (SMIL repaints on the main thread every frame: on a 4x-throttled phone the flutter took most of a page at rest, docs/PERFORMANCE.md): a SMIL `<animate>` on the pennant's `d` steps through 8 shapes of a wave travelling along the cloth (`pennantWave`, amplitude 2.2u, a 2.6 s loop whose last frame is its first), so it runs in every browser; the edge at the pole never moves, and the pole and the ball stay still. The folds move with it: each of the fold gradient's stops carries its own `<animate>` on `stop-color` through the same 8 frames (`clothStops` at each frame's phase), and the light-and-shade gradient follows the shape's box, so the bands travel along the cloth with the wave. Under `prefers-reduced-motion` (`useReducedMotion` in `Logo.tsx`) every `<animate>` is left out and the pennant keeps its resting shape and folds. The tile mark, the favicon, the app icons and the share card never wave.
- The line and the ball share one easing (`cubic-bezier(.25, .65, .3, 1)`) and 1.2 s.
- When: on the Home hero once per page load (0.15 s delay; going back to Home later does not replay it), and on the top-bar lockup on hover (pointer devices) or keyboard focus.

Components (`Logo.tsx`):

```tsx
<Lockup height={40} animate="hover" />   // the top bar (32 px on a phone, set through --h in styles.css)
<Lockup height={104} animate="load" />   // the Home hero, inside the h1 (72 px on a phone)
<Logo size="lg" />                       // the compact lockup, tile + name: the loading screen and tight headers
<LogoMark size={72} />                   // the mark alone
<LogoMark size="md" variant="mono" title="TouchLine" />
<Wordmark />                             // "Touch" + "Line" in the accent blue, real text
```

`Lockup` takes `height` in px (it picks the ball's tier; a stylesheet may resize it through `--h`) and `animate` (`none`, `load`, `hover`). `LogoMark` sizes: `sm` 24, `md` 32, `lg` 48, `xl` 96, or any number of pixels; the tier follows the size. In the compact `Logo` the wordmark is 0.82 × the mark's size, with a gap of 0.3 × the mark.

Variants: `colour` (the blue tile; the default and the app icon), `mono` (no tile, everything in `currentColor`, the ball's panels knocked out of a solid disc: for light ink on a photo or a primary button), `inverse` (a white tile with the pole, the line and an outlined ball in the tile blue, the pennant red: on a blue or navy background, and the pattern to follow for dark ink).

Rules:

- Clear space around the mark: at least a quarter of its size; around the lockup, at least the ball's diameter (23u). Smallest sizes: the mark 16 px (the favicon), the lockup 32 px high.
- Never recolour the tile or the pennant, stretch either, outline the tile, put the colour tile on the primary blue (use `inverse`), add grass or a second ball, or set the name in capitals or with a space.
- The mark is decorative next to the wordmark (`aria-hidden`); give `LogoMark` a `title` when it stands alone. In the lockup only the name is read; the shine copy, the flag and the ball are hidden.
- SVG ids are prefixed per instance (`useId`), so two logos on a screen never share a gradient or a clip.
- After any change to `MARK`, run `node scripts/brand/icons.mjs` (Node 22.18+ and Playwright; set `PLAYWRIGHT_MODULE` to Playwright's entry file if it is not installed in the project). It writes `public/favicon.svg` (the tiny tier), `icons/icon-192.png` and `icon-512.png` (full tier, rounded tile, transparent corners), `icons/icon-maskable-512.png` (full bleed, glyph at 78 % inside the safe circle) and `icons/apple-touch-icon.png` (full bleed, opaque).
- The share card (`shareCard.ts`) draws the open lockup (the short line, the resting pennant with its folds, the pole and its finial, no line under the name) with `Path2D` from `LOCKUP`, `pennantPath` and `ballPanels`, 76 px high, in the Floodlit colours of `BRAND_COLOURS`, whatever the viewer's theme.

## 3. Colour

Two themes share every token name: **Floodlit** (dark, "Dark" in the settings) and **Daylight** (light, "Light", `[data-theme='light']`). Only use the names; the values change with the theme.

The profile's theme setting is Auto, Light or Dark (Profile screen). `App.tsx` always sets `data-theme` on `<html>` to what is on screen: the fixed choice, or for Auto the device's `prefers-color-scheme`, followed live (`useResolvedTheme` listens to the media query, so switching the phone to dark mode switches the game at once). A small script in `index.html` sets the same attribute before the app loads, so a light device never flashes the dark page. The CSS default with no attribute is still Floodlit. The sun and moon button in the top bar flips Light and Dark and stores the result, so after one tap the setting is fixed until Auto is chosen again. The browser's `theme-color` follows `--bg`.

## 4. Typography

One family: **Sora** (Google Fonts, SIL Open Font License; 400, 600, 700, 800 from one stylesheet in `index.html`) for titles, big numbers, the call to action and body text alike: a wide geometric sans with flat, squared terminals, our free stand-in for the look of a European club-competition broadcast face (the real broadcast fonts are exclusive and are never used). `--font-display` and `--font-body` both name it, so a rule can still say which role it plays; the fallbacks are `'Segoe UI', system-ui, -apple-system, Roboto, sans-serif`. Figures (minutes, ratings, scores, stats, the match clock) are Sora too: `--mono` names `--font-body`, and every rule that sets it (and the `.mono` class) turns on tabular figures, so columns line up without a second face (2026-10-28: it was the system monospace, which outside a Mac drew DejaVu Sans Mono or Consolas beside Sora on the match day and the player card). `--code` keeps a true monospace for what a reader types or copies: seed fields, room codes, links and share text (`.input.mono`, `pre.mono`, `.chip.mono`, `.btn.mono`, `kbd`). Sora is about 45 % wider than the old condensed display face, so display sizes were cut where a box is fixed (below). The share cards draw in Sora too and wait (up to 2 s) for `document.fonts` before drawing; `public/sw.js` caches the Google Fonts stylesheet and files cache-first, so the type survives offline after one visit.

Rules:

- **Mixed case everywhere.** `h1` to `h4`, panel titles, buttons (the big ones too), the scoreboard and the tier banner are written in sentence case with no `text-transform`. Capitals are only for `.eyebrow`, `.stat .label`, table headers, position and club codes, chips that hold a code, and the scoreboard clock ("HALF TIME").
- **Tier names** come from the engine in capitals. Display them with `tierName(tier)` ("Title race", "38-0: The perfect season"). The share card already does; the tier banner, the leaderboard and head-to-head move to it in the screen pass.
- **Numbers that matter are big and bold**: scores, points, prices, ratings, stat values (`.stat .value` is 24 px display, no wrap). Scores, stat values, the record line, the hero stats and table `.num` cells use `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` so figures line up. Other display sizes set for the wider face: the big button 17 px, the tier banner 44 px, the record 30 px, the scoreboard 46 px (team codes 19 px), the reel's club 19 px and codes 20 px.
- Line height 1.45 for body text, 1.1 for titles, 1 for big numbers.
- On phones, inputs are 16 px so iOS does not zoom (already global).
- **Sizes are tokens, never fixed px** (rem, so they follow iOS Dynamic Type, and the tokens step up about a fifth from 2200x1200, section 13). A size between the steps is the nearest token plus or minus a pixel or two (`calc(var(--fs-base) + 1px)` for 15 px, `calc(var(--fs-xs) - 1px)` for 10 px), and a display size a multiple of a token (`calc(var(--fs-h1) * 1.25)` for 40 px), so it is the same at 100 % and grows with the scale. Only text drawn to a fixed geometry keeps px, each with a `/* px: ... */` note: the pitch discs, their labels and the mini pitches, the live pitch's caption and REPLAY pill, the reel and the item reels' cards, the rating badge boxes, the form boxes, the game-by-game and monogram badges, the Trophy Room rings, the recap grade disc, the slot sheet's disc, the Upgrader dial, the player card's SVG chart text, the match-day formation and substitution pitches, the 16 px phone inputs (iOS zoom), and the fluid `clamp()` sizes of the goal burst. (2026-10-26: about 270 px sizes moved; `font-size: Npx` without a note is a bug, and since 2026-10-27 `src/ui/__tests__/cssGuards.test.ts` fails CI on one; it also keeps an inline `fontSize` literal in a component to a badge box, so an inline size is a token too: `calc(var(--fs-base) + 2px)`, not `16`.)

## 6. Components

All of these are global classes or components already in the code; screens compose them.

### Buttons (`.btn`)

| Primary | `.btn.primary` | The one forward action of a region: "Spin the wheel", "Kick off the season", "Continue →" |
| Secondary | `.btn` (or `.btn.secondary`) | Alternatives and everyday actions: "Play friendly", "Sim 5", "Download share card" |

|---|---|---|

### Segmented controls (`Segmented`, `.segmented`)

For two to five mutually exclusive options with labels of one or two words. The active option takes the selection pattern (`--primary` border, `--primary-soft` fill). Put the explanation in the option's `hint` (a title) or in an `Info` beside the field label, never in a sentence under the control. On a phone the options wrap; seven leagues is the most a segmented control should carry. Beyond that, use a select or a picture grid.

### Fields: inputs, selects and toggles

```tsx
<label className="field">
  Team name
  <input className="input" … />
</label>

<div className="field">
  <span>Difficulty <Info>Calibrated scales the real clubs so an average draft finishes mid-table.</Info></span>
  <Segmented … />
</div>

<label className="toggle">
  <input type="checkbox" … /> Hard mode <Info>Ratings stay hidden while you draft.</Info>
</label>
```

- `.field` (a `label` or a `div`) stacks the label (13 px, 600, `--text-2`), the control, and at most one `.help` caption.
- Native checkboxes, radios and sliders take `--primary` through `accent-color` on `body`.
- `.input` and `select.input` are full width, 40 px, on `--field-bg` with the 3:1 `--field-border`; focus shows the `--focus` ring.
- `.toggle` is a checkbox row; checked toggles take the selection pattern. The label is one line of six words or fewer. `.toggle.era-classic` is the compact form for a panel head row (2 px by 10 px padding, 13 px text, no minimum height): the Setup Era panel's Classic toggle.
- Two fields side by side only on desktop (`.grid.two`); on a phone everything stacks.

### Info and captions: the rule for explanatory text

The Setup screen showed the problem: a helper sentence under every control. The rule now:

1. **A control gets a label, nothing else, by default.**
2. If it needs explaining, add **one `<Info>`** beside its label: a sentence or two, plain text, no links.
3. If the user needs one fact to choose well (the current value's consequence), use **one `.help` caption** of at most one line on a phone (about 60 characters). Never more than one caption per control, and preferably no more than one per panel.
4. **Never a paragraph under every control.** Paragraphs belong to How it works.
5. A panel's purpose goes in its title; a subtitle in `.panel-head` is at most eight words.
6. `.notice` is for state that changes what the user can do (today's puzzle, a challenge, the window being shut, an error), never for static explanation.
7. Show numbers instead of sentences: "Wage room £1.2M/wk", not "You have £1.2M a week of room under the wage cap".

`Info` (bits.tsx): `<Info label="About seeds">Share a seed and a friend gets the same wheel.</Info>`. A mouse opens it on hover, a tap toggles it, keyboard focus opens it; Escape, a tap elsewhere, scrolling or the mouse leaving closes it. The tip is portalled to `<body>`, positioned with `position: fixed` and clamped to the viewport, so it is never clipped by a scrolling list and never widens the page; it is `role="tooltip"` and linked with `aria-describedby` while open. `label` is the button's accessible name (default "More information"). Inside a `<label>` the button's name joins the control's name, so keep `label` short, or use a `div.field` and give the control its own `aria-label`.

`InfoTip` (PowerUpButton.tsx) is the smaller "i" of Setup and the power-up tray: a mouse shows it while over the button, a click, tap or Enter keeps it open, Escape closes it and gives the focus back, a press elsewhere closes it. Its bubble (`.qinfo-bubble`) is portalled and fixed beside the button at 280 to 340 px (`tipWidth`, `placeTip`), so a box that scrolls or clips never squeezes it. `HoverTips` wraps a row of controls that each carry a `data-tip`: one `.info-tip` under the hovered, keyboard-focused or tapped control (the Setup modes row, test id `mode-tip`). Use it instead of a bare `title`, which shows late, never on a tap and never on focus.

### Ratings: overall and in a slot

A player has one rating people know and one number per slot; every screen names which it is showing (`src/game/logic/slotRating.ts`, pure):

- **Overall**: the `Rating` badge on a player row, the player card and every list (its tooltip reads "Overall 89"). It never changes with the slot.
- **In a slot**: his overall less the out-of-position cost (`discRating`: overall times the fit factor). It is the number on his pitch disc, and the headline of every slot picker: the Draft's slot chips (`RW 84 · can cover (89 overall, −5 out of position)`; `LW 89 · fits` in a natural position), the Dugout's slot sheet (the number on the right, the `−5` under it) and the end case's Icon chips. On the pitch an out-of-position disc carries a small `−N` badge (`.disc-cost`, its border the fit colour) and the tooltip "84 at RW (89 overall, −5 out of position)". In his natural position the two numbers are the same, so a card, a chip and a disc never disagree without saying why.
- **Attributes for a position** (`ratingAt`): TouchLine's own weighting of his attributes for that position, which orders the slot sheet and the end case. It is never printed bare: only in a chip's tooltip, as "Attributes for RW: 75". (Before 2026-10-26 the Draft chip printed it as the headline, so Özil read 89 on his row, "RW 80" on the chip and 89 on the disc.)

- The draft recap, the pick grades and the Hindsight XI count a man **in a slot** too (`inSlotRating` in `pickGrades.ts`, the disc's number), so "Best pick: 84 at CB" is the 84 on his disc.

## 7. Imagery

"More pictures" means our own imagery, never anyone's branding. The owner's call: fun cartoon sketches, not real photos. There are no photos in the game.

- **Allowed**: SVG we draw (the cartoon scenes and the neon flow below, pitches, formation diagrams, the mark), `ClubBadge` colour discs, drawn flags, league marks (a disc in the league colour with its three-letter code, from `LEAGUE_COLOURS` in `leagueSegments.ts`), charts drawn from game data, Lucide icons.
- **Never**: photos or downloaded art, real club crests, kits, league or competition logos, player photos or likenesses, any identifiable face, sponsor boards or stadium names, screenshots of other games.
- **Never anything that could pass as UEFA or Champions League branding.** The palette is inspired by night-football broadcast colours (navy, cyan, magenta), in our own design: no starball, no star shapes, star patterns or ball-of-stars, no "UCL" or "Champions League" text, no UEFA photos or trade dress. The art uses discs, rounded points and curves, never stars (the crown's points are rounded and its gems are discs). League and club names stay as they are.

## 12. Motion

The owner asked for "some site animation" and "some nice touchup". Motion here is feedback and a sense of place, never decoration that waits on the player: short, once, and on the compositor.

### Tokens

| Token | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--dur-1` | 150 ms | Hover colours, the press-down (60 ms while held), the outgoing screen |
| `--dur-2` | 220 ms | Toasts leaving, modals and the scrim on a desktop, the old screen in a View Transition |
| `--dur-3` | 320 ms | The screen entrance, the tab ink, a phone sheet sliding up, a toast arriving, the theme icon |
| `--dur-4` | 400 ms | Rises on Home, bars growing, the tier pop |
| `--ease-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.7, 0.2, 1)` | Everything that arrives or moves |
| `--ease-in-out` | `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 0.2, 1)` | Things that leave, the loading sweep |
| `--ease-spring` | `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.9, 0.3, 1.25)` | A small overshoot for something worth noticing: the toast, the tier, the theme icon |
| `--stagger` | 60 ms | The step between items entering one after another |

`DURATION` in `src/ui/motion.ts` mirrors the four durations for the JavaScript side (the count-up runs for `DURATION.slow`).

### Rules

1. **150 to 400 ms**, on the tokens. The exceptions are named: the reel and the wheel (their timing is part of the draft: about 7.2 s to the reveal, 2.7 s on a quick spin or a re-spin, `src/ui/reelCurve.ts`; see CLAUDE.md), the live match pitch, the hero art's settle (`hero-in`) and the travelling neon light (`neon-run`, 7 s, the only loop; ambient, nothing waits on them), the lockup's own load and hover animation (section 2), the loading sweep (a 1.4 s loop, only while the packs load), the account chip's sync spinner (a 1.2 s turn while a sync runs), the January deadline ticker (a 30 s marquee; reduced motion shows it still, ellipsed) and the celebration (0.9 s, once per result). A flash or pop that must last longer than 400 ms is written as a multiple of a token (`calc(var(--dur-4) * 3)` for the 38-0 chase breaking, `calc(var(--dur-2) * 5)` for the scoreboard flash), and curves are `--ease-*`, never `ease` or a hand-written `cubic-bezier` (only the named exceptions keep their own). A cup night's reveal is pacing, not an animation: each tie's landing runs on the tokens, and the wait between ties is `CUP_REVEAL` in `src/ui/cupReveal.ts` (Skip ends it).
2. **Transform and opacity only.** No animated width, height, top or left, so nothing reflows. Bars grow with `scaleX`, the tab ink moves with `translate` and `scaleX`, the toast keeps its centring `translate` in every frame. The one width transition left is a bar's value changing in place (an old rule, on one small element). Something placed in per cent of a box (the match day's formation men) is placed by a transform in container units: the box is a size container and the man's `translate(calc(var(--x) * 1cqw - 50%), …)` moves, not his `left` and `top` (`cssGuards.test.ts` fails on a `left` or `top` transition).
3. **Once.** Entrances run when something mounts and never loop. Entrances use `animation-fill-mode: backwards`, so a finished animation never pins a transform over `:hover`.
4. **Never block.** Nothing the player or a smoke script waits for is hidden behind an animation; buttons work while things are still rising. Count-ups mark themselves with `data-counting` while they climb.
5. **Reduced motion turns it all off.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` the motion section's last block stops every entrance, transition, hover lift and press; `withViewTransition` and `useCountUp` land at once; the celebration is not drawn; the eligible-slot pulse on the pitch stops. Add every new animated selector to that block.
6. **No dependency.** CSS first; the few helpers in `motion.ts` are plain React. No animation library.
7. **Decoration never touches the game.** No helper reads the game's RNG or writes state; the confetti is a fixed pattern (`confettiPieces`, the golden angle), not random numbers.

## 14. Sound

TouchLine's sounds are synthesised in the browser with the Web Audio API: oscillators, FM pairs, gain envelopes and bands of seeded noise. No audio file ships and no recording, sample or game sound is copied. The code is `src/ui/sound/`:

- `scores.ts` (pure): the palette. Every sound is a score, a list of voices (`tone`, `fm`, `noise`), each with a start, a linear attack, a hold and an exponential fall. `scoreFor(request)` builds one, the same every time for the same request (cosmetic variation comes from `seeded` in `noise.ts`, never `Math.random` or the game's RNG).
- `noise.ts` (pure): seeded white and pink noise, each buffer scaled to a peak of exactly 1, and the small `seeded` generator.
- `render.ts`: a score into Web Audio nodes on any context (the live one, or an `OfflineAudioContext` to render a file), the looped noise bank and the safety net.
- `match.ts` (pure): when the match-day dashboard sounds (`matchSoundCues`, `goalMoments`, `dueMatchSounds`); `useMatchSounds.ts` the dashboard's one hook.
- `seasonScores.ts` (pure): the season's cues as scores, in the palette's voices (`scaleTo` normalises them as `normalise` does); `seasonCues.ts` (pure) when they play (`playbackCues`, `seasonStepCues`); `usePlaybackSounds.ts` the quick playback's hook; `seasonWatch.ts` Season and Dynasty's store watcher. All four load with the playback and the Dugout, never in the first download.
- `synth.ts`: the context, `MASTER_GAIN` and `gainNow` (the master at the viewer's volume), the gesture unlock, `playSound(request, delay)` (a palette sound or a cue, through the category switch and the thinning), `playScore(score, delay)` for a score built elsewhere (on the master, so the volume applies, through the score's own `category` and its gap under `thinAs`: the season's cues are `season:<id>`), and the reel's tick, whoosh and pop (palette scores, played at the strip's times on the Draft switch; the Upgrader's dial at most one tick per `DIAL_TICK_GAP_MS`, 15 ms); `reel.ts` one reel run; `schedule.ts` (pure) the reel's tick times; `prefs.ts` the switches and the volume (`categoryOn`); `boot.ts` arms it all once from `App.tsx`.
- `cues.ts` (pure, second pass, 2026-10-27): the cues beside the palette (`CueRequest`), the switch each follows (`CUE_CATEGORY`), the thinning (`CUE_GAP_MS`, `makeThinner`) and the screens' small decisions: `pickBand` (a pick's band from his overall), `toastTone` (an error or a plain toast, from its words), `newlyEarned` (the Trophy Room's new badges). `voices.ts` (pure) their scores (`cueScore`, `CUE_LEVEL`), built from the palette's blocks and normalised the same way; a chunk of its own, imported only with `import()` by `synth.ts` on the first gesture, so the first download carries none of it (`soundCues.test.ts` fails on a static import). `useTurnSound.ts` (a head-to-head turn) and `useBadgeSound.ts` (the Trophy Room) are the two hooks.

| An arena win | Sound only | A run won, on its summary, once per visit (`ArenaSummary`) | The Cup's fanfare (the palette's), 0.6 s in |
| A badge | Sound only | The Trophy Room showing a badge or tier earned since this viewer last heard one (`useBadgeSound`, key `touchline-badges-heard`, also on every summary's unlock card), one ring however many | The trophy unlock: a low strike, a quick C major run of mallets, a round C6 bell held over its fifth on a pad, a glint. 0.43 |
| The volume | Sound only | The Volume slider let go (Profile) | The pick's two mallets at the pick's level, the everyday sound to set the level by, whichever switch is off. 0.19 |


### The ball and the stadium (round 8)

The ball's own sounds on the live pitch (`ball.ts`, pure; `useBallSounds.ts`, the hook inside `LivePitch`), read from the same keyframes the pitch draws and played as the shared live clock passes them, on the Match switch. The match day and a quick season's highlights reel both carry them (`LivePitch`'s `sounds` prop); the reel, which has no goal holds, also plays a goal's net and roar as the ball arrives, toned for the user's side.

| Cue | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| A touch, a pass, a long ball | As the ball leaves a keyframe (a set piece when it is taken, at `setPiece.until`): a dribble or a carry is a touch, a long ball, switch, cross, clearance or distribution is a long ball | A boot's low thump (a sine falling an octave from 140, 120 or 95 Hz) under the slap of leather (a 20 to 35 ms band of noise); a long ball adds the ball's short whoosh. 0.07 / 0.1 / 0.14 |
| A strike | As the ball leaves the shooter's keyframe on a hop that ends in the net, on the keeper or wide | A firmer thump from 85 Hz, a brighter slap, a crack of high noise and the whoosh. 0.24 |
| A header, a throw, a goal kick | Each as it is taken | A header duller (no slap above 700 Hz), a throw only the hands' slap, a goal kick a long ball's. 0.12 / 0.06 / 0.15 |
| The keeper's gloves | A save arriving in his hands | A padded clap: a thump from 150 Hz and a low-passed slap. 0.22 (beside the save's own "ooh" at the strike) |
| The woodwork | A shot arriving on the post or the bar (the hop that comes back off it, play `rebound`) | The thud and four inharmonic partials of a steel tube (523, 1347, 2389, 3712 Hz) ringing out over a second. 0.34 |
| A swell | An attack first getting into the last fifth of the pitch (`DANGER_FROM` 78) | The crowd rising over half a second and falling back unanswered, louder the deeper the ball. 0.12 to 0.3, at most one each 2.5 s |
| The crowd's bed | While the clock runs | A low murmur and a band of distant voices breathing slowly, 4.6 s each, laid end over end every 3.6 s (`BED_EVERY_MS`); a pause, a closed board or sound off stops it at once. 0.12 (0.6 of that in the reel) |

Thinning (`dueBallCues`): an ordinary touch only `BALL_GAP_MS` (350 ms of real time) after the last one that sounded, so a busy spell never machine-guns; strikes, saves and the woodwork always sound; from 3x only the long balls and those; every kick quieter at speed (`speedScale`: 0.75 at 2x, 0.55 from 3x); nothing through a goal's hold (the net and the roar have it), a skip (more than a minute in one step) or a step back. Tested in `src/ui/__tests__/ballSounds.test.ts` against real match paths.

The round also enriched the palette's own voices, keeping every level and the whistle's carrier (smokes still tell it by 2950 × 0.92 Hz): the whistle's ring band now rattles with the pea (a 46 Hz tremolo) and each blast opens on a short hiss of breath; the goal's roar turns to applause (a band of claps with a fast, deep tremolo) over a low stadium tail that rolls on and dies away last (left out of the Treble's, which stays under 6 s); the "ooh" settles into a low murmur.

The reel's own sounds: a tick each time a card crosses the marker (round 9: a 30 ms knock of noise band-passed near 2 kHz over a tiny tock falling 760 to 600 Hz, where it was a 3.2 kHz click; pitch varied by about 8 %, timed from the strip's position function and the measured card boundaries, both ways, capped at one per 15 ms and stopped on a skip; on the Upgrader's dial, one per mark, at most one per 15 ms), a whoosh as the season drum rolls (pink noise swept 280 Hz to 1.7 kHz) and a pop as it stops (a sine 420 to 170 Hz over a felt knock).

### The season's cues

1. **One master, normalised scores.** The master gain is 0.35 (`MASTER_GAIN`). Each score is normalised so its bound, the most its voices can add up to at any moment, is exactly its level, and no level is above `MAX_LEVEL` (0.9). In the bound an oscillator counts in full; a noise band counts by `sourceBound`, the most its filter lets through of noise peaking at 1 (measured over the very buffers the game uses, and checked against the Web Audio biquads by `soundPalette.test.ts`); independent noise bands add in quadrature, as their peaks do. Levels set the balance: the goal's roar is the loudest match sound, the whistle well under it, the landings rise from blue to gold, a click is barely there.
2. **Never clips.** The loudest sound peaks near −14 dBFS in the offline renders; even two at full level stay under the safety net's knee. The net (`safetyNet`, a waveshaper after the master: the identity up to 0.8, then a soft bend that never reaches 0.98) only acts on a pile of sounds at once. No compressor: its make-up gain would move every sound.
3. **No clicks.** Every envelope starts and ends on silence (0.0001), and its gain is silent before its first event too.

### Rules

1. **After a gesture.** The context is created or resumed only inside a real (trusted) pointer, key or touch gesture, and only once the page has had one (`navigator.userActivation`); `boot.ts` listens for them app-wide in the capture phase, so the Spin click, the Play button before a live match and a primary button all unlock it. Nothing plays before; a script's synthetic clicks never do.
2. **On by default, the viewer's choice.** Sound (the speaker beside the reel, `sound-toggle`, and Sound in Profile, `sound-on` / `sound-off`) is the master switch, per device under `touchline-sound`: off creates no context and plays nothing. Under it, shown while Sound is on, Profile has the Volume (`sound-volume`, 0 to 100 % in steps of 5, `touchline-sound-volume`: the master is `MASTER_GAIN` times `volumeGain`, the slider's share to the power 1 / 0.6 (Stevens' law, so each step sounds like the same step louder: half way is about −10 dB, 10 % about −33 dB), never louder than the first pass) and three category switches: Match sounds (`match-sound-on` / `-off`, `touchline-match-sound`: the whistle, the crowd, the net, cards, substitutions, the arena's kicks and a bracket's ties), Draft sounds (`draft-sound-on` / `-off`, `touchline-draft-sound`: the reel's ticks, whoosh, pop and landing, a pick, the Upgrader, the grade, the Hindsight XI, Squad complete, the manager spin, the case, a bonus spin and a head-to-head turn) and Interface sounds (`ui-sound-on` / `-off`, `touchline-ui-sound`: the click, a tab, a toast, an error, a confirm). The fanfares, a Gauntlet rung, a badge, a season's stingers and closing fanfare and the volume's own note are rewards and follow Sound alone; a season's results, cheers, riffles, crowd bed and January chime are match sounds (round 9; they followed Sound alone before). None of it is in the save.
3. **In time with the picture.** The match's sounds read the same clock as the live pitch (`timeline` over `useLiveClock`) every frame: a cue sounds when the clock passes it, a goal when its hold passes the ball crossing the line. A clock that goes back (a change in play) plays nothing, a skip plays only the last whistle it passes, the crowd never comes closer than 350 ms in a fast-forward, and the stretch since the last frame is still heard when the whistle closes the dashboard.
4. **Thinned on rapid repeats.** A sound with a gap in `CUE_GAP_MS` (every cue, the click and the pick: 70 ms for a click, 110 ms for a tab, 600 ms for a toast, up to 1.5 s for a badge; since round 9 also a landing 150 ms, the Upgrader 500 ms, the grade 1 s, a fanfare 1.5 s, and the season's cues under `season:<id>`) plays at most once inside it; a repeat inside is dropped, not queued, and does not push the window on. The match's own cues keep their own throttle (rule 3).
5. **Reduced motion keeps sound.** It drops the shimmers and the reel's ticks (there is no slide), and a goal's net sounds the moment its still frame shows (there is no flight and no replay). The second pass does the same: its shimmers (a world-class pick, the Hindsight XI's glint, Squad complete, a bonus spin, a badge) go and the rest stays, and a shoot-out revealed at once sounds its last kick. `prefers-reduced-data` changes nothing: no sound is downloaded.
6. **Silent without Web Audio.** No `AudioContext` (tests, headless runs without one, old browsers) makes every call a no-op that returns nothing.
7. **Presentation only.** Nothing reads or writes the game's RNG or state; the match's sounds only read the clock and the events the dashboard already shows. `Math.random` only varies a reel tick's pitch (`reel.ts`); the tick's noise is the seeded bank's.
8. **Hear them.** The offline renders (one WAV per sound) come from a small harness that bundles `scores.ts` and `render.ts` and runs them in an `OfflineAudioContext` through the same master and safety net.

While a season simulates, quiet cues in the same voices (`seasonScores.ts`, levels 0.09 to 0.45, every one at most half a goal's roar; the 38 result blips at a click's level). `seasonCues.ts` decides them from what the screen already shows; `prefers-reduced-motion` drops their sparkles only.

| Cue | When | How |
|---|---|---|
| A result | Each game revealed in the quick playback; Play matchweek on Sim result | A win a rising fourth (E5, A5), a draw one mellow A4, a loss a falling pair (E4, C♯4), all under 0.6 s |
| The goals | Under a result with two goals or more, never under a defeat | A short lift of the crowd (pink noise), a little bigger and longer per goal up to five; never a roar |
| A stinger | The game that brings it, the heaviest one only: the title sealed, the drop certain (`clinchMatchweeks`), the perfect run over, top of the table, into the top four, still perfect every fifth win (`momentAt`) | Title a brass "ta-daa" on a timpani stroke; relegation a dark low chord sinking; the run over a soft falling minor third; top a C major arpeggio; top four three rising notes; still perfect a bright arpeggio |
| The crowd bed | The last five games of a perfect run, at 2x and slower | A low rumble with a faint A2, swelling over the gap to the next result and louder nearer the end; the next result or a pause cuts it |
| The season's end | The last game of the playback | A short fanfare by the final place: champions brass into a held chord (a perfect season adds a timpani roll), the top four an arpeggio, mid-table a soft F to C cadence, the drop a low minor one. Shorter and quieter than the Cup's |
| January | The first half shown (the window opens) | A soft rising fourth, G5 then C6 |
| A run of results | Skip in the playback; Sim 5, Sim to end and Sim to the next big match (the digest) | One riffle of up to six pips in the run's proportions, then a closing interval by points a game; the title or relegation stinger after it when the run settled it |

Pace: at 4x (375 ms a game, under `FAST_BELOW_MS`) a result is a shorter, quieter blip, never closer than 700 ms to the last unless the outcome changed, a stinger takes its place, and there is no cheer and no bed. Skip plays one riffle and the closing cue, never the skipped games. Pausing (or opening a game's highlights) stops whatever is still sounding; the playback reaching its stop does not, so the fanfare rings out. A Season week watched or auto-played is the match day's (its whistles): only a week played without the dashboard sounds here, and a season that ends with the title leaves it to the summary's fanfare.

### Loudness

The balance (round 9). A score's bound (its level) is a safe ceiling, not its loudness: it counts a noise band heavily and every partial in full, so two scores at one level can sound 10 dB apart (the first round-9 renders had the pick at −39 dB beside a landing at −29). So every level outside the match day was set from offline renders (each score through `MASTER_GAIN` and the safety net in Chromium's `OfflineAudioContext`, 48 kHz; the loudest 100 ms window's RMS, dBFS) to these steps, and the tests keep the orderings (`soundRound9.test.ts`):

| Step | Sounds (loudest 100 ms) |
|---|---|
| The interface | a click −56 and a tab −58 (each about 30 ms, peaks near −38), a toast, a confirm and an error −43 to −44 |
| The reel's rattle | the tick −55 (30 ms), the whoosh −45, the pop −43 |
| The season ticking over | a result −40 to −41, a riffle −37, a stinger −32, the season's end −32 |
| The draft, every day | a pick (and the volume's note) −34, a turn −33, the case −34, the manager spin −32, the Hindsight XI −32, a rung −31, a bracket's tie −38 (another's −50) |
| The landings | blue −33, purple −31.5, pink −30, red −28, gold −26 |
| The draft's moments | a world-class pick −30, the grade A −29 to D −33, the Upgrader −28 won and −31 lost, Squad complete and a bonus spin −30 |
| The trophies | a badge −28, the Cup −26.6, the Treble −26.4: level with a goal's roar (−26.6), the loudest of the match day |

The warmth is tested too: no tone starts in under 1.5 ms, each sound's tones weigh in under 1.6 kHz (under 1 kHz on average), and glints above 2 kHz never carry a fifth of a sound.

## 15. Accessibility

What the game supports, the audit and what is left are in [ACCESSIBILITY.md](ACCESSIBILITY.md). The rules for a new component or screen (first pass 2026-10-26, second pass the same day):

1. **A real control, or one that acts like it.** Anything pressed is a `<button>`, a link or a form field. Where only another element can sit (a table row), spread `pressable(action)` from `src/ui/a11y.ts` on it: Tab reaches it and Enter or Space presses it. A custom `role="button"` answers both keys (`isActivationKey`), and only when it can be pressed: a read-only picture takes no Tab stop.
2. **Every control has a name.** An icon-only button carries `aria-label` (the Lucide icon inside hides itself); a select or input has a `<label>` or an `aria-label`; a group of segments or radios sits in a named `role="group"` or `role="radiogroup"`. `aria-pressed` is for toggle buttons; a `role="radio"` uses `aria-checked` only.
3. **A label needs a role.** `aria-label` on a plain `<span>` or `<div>` is ignored by screen readers (axe: `aria-prohibited-attr`): give a mark that stands alone `role="img"` (a badge, an arrow, a grade, an icon with a label) and a box of parts `role="group"`. Key-value pairs are a `<dl className="kv">`.
4. **Never colour alone.** A result shows its W, D or L beside the colour (`.res-letter`, the form letters); text on a club's colour takes `inkOn()` or `textColourFor()`, which always clear 4.5:1; a new colour pair is checked against both themes (section 3). Dimming text with `opacity` takes it under AA: mark an unavailable thing `aria-disabled` or use a dashed border instead.
5. **Announce once, politely, from a region that is already there.** A live region that mounts with its text is often missed: keep an `sr-only` `aria-live="polite"` (or `role="status"`) element in the page and change its text. Say the moment, not the stream: a goal, a landing, a big moment, a toast; never a count-up or every game of a playback. A region that must stay audible behind a dialog carries `data-dialog-keep`.
6. **Move the focus when the control that held it goes.** A reel's Skip button vanishes on landing: `useFocusOnLanding(landed, ref)` puts the focus on the result's first action (or the landed club's name, `tabIndex={-1}`, no ring of its own). A dialog uses `Modal` or `useDialog` (section 6), never its own focus code. Behind both, the focus rescue (`src/ui/focusRescue.ts`, round 3) puts a focus whose control left the page on the first control of the nearest box still there (a dialog, a `.panel`, a `.fit-cell`), or on the new screen's page; it is the fallback, so a component that knows the next step (Spin after a pick, Done after the trophy) still says so with `useFocusOnLanding`, which treats a rescued focus as fallen.
7. **A box that scrolls is reachable.** A new box that scrolls inside itself either holds a control or joins `SCROLL_BOXES` in `src/ui/scrollFocus.ts`, which puts it in the Tab order while it scrolls.
8. **Tabs are one Tab stop.** A `role="tablist"` takes `onKeyDown={onTabListKey}` (`src/ui/a11y.ts`) and its tabs `tabIndex={selected ? 0 : -1}`: Tab enters on the chosen tab, the arrows, Home and End move and choose. `Tabs` in `bits.tsx` already does.
   A radio group is one Tab stop too (round 4): the `role="radiogroup"` takes `onKeyDown={onRadioGroupKey}` and each radio `tabIndex={radioTabIndex(i, checkedIndex)}`; `Segmented` and `ChoiceCards` already do, and a `Segmented` takes `label` (what is chosen: "Sound", "Where to play"), since its On and Off alone say nothing; `label` is required (round 5), so TypeScript refuses an unnamed one, and `src/ui/__tests__/a11yRound5.test.ts` reads every `.tsx` under `src/ui` and fails on an unnamed `Segmented`, an unnamed `role="radiogroup"` or an icon-only button without an `aria-label`. A button never holds another: a row with buttons of its own (Explore's compare and shortlist) passes them to `PlayerRow`'s `actions`, which draws them beside the row's button.
9. **Hover is never the only way.** A hover card or tip also opens from a tap or the keyboard (or its content is in the full card a tap opens) and closes on Escape.
10. **Targets on a phone.** At 720 px and below every control is 44 px tall: `.btn` (and `.btn.sm`), `.chip-btn`, `.segmented` buttons, `.tabs` buttons, `.input` and `select.input` already are, so a new control built from them needs nothing. A round icon button or an inline link keeps its look and grows an invisible `::after` (as `.info-btn`, `.theme-switch`, `.reel-sound` and `.linklike` do), kept clear of its neighbour's; nothing is under 24 px.
   At every width a small inline control (the setup cards' `.qinfo-btn`, `.linklike`, `.boxes-btn`, a `button.chip`, `.fold-head`, a `summary.panel-head`) keeps its look but takes a 24 px hit area from an `::after` (WCAG 2.2 target size); the inset counts from inside the border (an 18 px bordered "i" needs 4 px), a control in a box that clips (the match day's crumb) is 24 px itself, a pressable pitch makes its disc the button (the slot's label room overlaps its team-mates'), and the match day's selects are 24 px tall and 16px on a phone (O-8 and O-12 in [QA.md](QA.md)).
11. **Larger text.** New type sizes use the `--fs-*` tokens (rem). A phone row with text must wrap or give way (`minmax(0, 1fr)`, `flex-wrap`, `overflow-wrap: anywhere` for a long capitalised label) at 200 % text (`html { font-size: 200% }`, how Dynamic Type arrives: `src/ui/textScale.ts`), and an absolutely placed `<svg>` gets an explicit width, since it takes its width from its height. A logotype or drawing with its own geometry (the lockup) stays in px. None of this may change the desktop fit at 100 %.
12. **Motion.** As section 12: every new animation is added to a reduced-motion block. Never fade text to show age or state (`opacity` under 1 takes it under AA): the playback's moment chip settles at full strength.
13. **One main landmark.** A screen's root is a `.page` placed directly in `.app`: `watchMainLandmark` (`src/ui/landmarks.ts`) gives it `role="main"` and the skip link's `id="main"`. Never add a `<main>` or another `role="main"` inside a screen; a screen panel is marked by its heading.

## 16. Voice and wording

- British English (colour, defence, favourite, -ise), sentence case for titles, buttons and tabs; the product is TouchLine (`APP_NAME`), the default team "Touchline XI".
- Mode names are proper names and keep their capitals in every label: Quick Draft, Daily Challenge, Weekly Challenge, Daily Arena, Super League, Run-in, Head-to-head, Trophy Room, Continental Cup. Squad First and Position First too.
- Numbers: figures for 10 and up, and for any count beside a unit ("Draft 18", "4 spins"); a count that can be one goes through `countPhrase` (`screenText.ts`) or a singular check: never "1 points".
- Minutes on the clock go through `minuteLabel` (45', 90+2'); a minutes total is "270 min". Money through `money` (£500k, £12.3M, £0), wages through `wage` (£85k/wk), a signed figure through `signedValue` (+0.4, −0.31, a true minus), meters through `meterValue`, formations through `formationName`, ages as "31y".
- A toast for a failure says what to do next ("Could not copy. Select the text manually.", "Could not make the share card. Try again."); a refusal says what would work ("Pick a highlighted slot.").
- An empty state says what fills it ("Spin the wheel; your best seasons land here.").
- Jargon (xG, Prime, chemistry, balance, the case tiers, Gamble) carries a tip or a title where it first appears on a screen; an `aria-label` contains the visible words (WCAG label in name).
- Never print an id (`arsenal@2015`, `4-4-2-diamond`): resolve it through `clubName`, `seasonLabel`, `formationName`.

## 17. Writing translatable copy

Every user-facing string goes through `t()` (`src/ui/i18n`), so the owner only has to pick languages and hand over
translations. English stays the source; nothing on screen changes in English.

- **Catalogues.** A namespace of English messages per screen or group, in `src/ui/i18n/en/<ns>.ts`
  (`catalog('home', { tagline: '…' })`), registered in `en/index.ts`. A file takes its `t` once at module level:
  `const t = translator(home)`. Keys are camelCase and say what the text is (`playDaily`, `dailyRanked`), never the
  English itself. TypeScript checks every key; `src/ui/__tests__/i18n.test.ts` fails on a key a call names that its
  catalogue lacks, a key nothing uses, and a message that does not parse. A key built at run time is a template with a
  fixed prefix (`` t(`tier_${tier}`) ``), so the test counts every key with that prefix as used.
- **One sentence, one message.** Never glue fragments (`'You have ' + n + ' spins'`): word order differs between
  languages. Put the values in as arguments: `'{n, plural, one {You have # spin} other {You have # spins}}'`. Bold or
  emphasis inside a sentence is a tag the translator can move: `'<b>Retired:</b> {names}'`, rendered by
  `rich(t('summerRetired', { names }), B)` (`src/ui/i18n/rich.tsx`); other elements get their own renderer
  (`rich(text, { flag: () => <LeagueFlag … /> })` with `<flag></flag>` in the message).
- **Plurals and variants in the message.** `{n, plural, one {…} other {…}}` (with `=0` for "none"), never an `s`
  added in code: other languages have more forms (Polish has four) and `Intl.PluralRules` picks them.
  `{venue, select, home {v} other {at}}` for a word that depends on a value; `{n, selectordinal, …}` for ordinal words.
- **Numbers, money and dates through `format.ts`.** `{m, money}`, `{k, wage}`, `{n, number}`, `{n, ordinal}`,
  `{d, date}` / `{d, date, long}`; in code, `fixed(n, 1)` instead of `n.toFixed(1)` where the figure is shown. English
  prints exactly what it printed before (`toFixed`, no grouping, so 2024 stays 2024); another language gets its own
  decimal mark and month names.
- **What is not translated.** `data-testid`, class names, storage keys and ids; data (player, club, nation and league
  names); the share text and the share card, which go to friends and stay English; and text the game logic or the
  engine builds (tier names, news, verdicts, rule chips). Those files are pure and some are bundled into the edge
  verifier (`src/engine/*`, the verifier's `src/game/logic` files), so their words stay put until a release moves them
  into the UI (as `src/ui/i18n/modeText.ts` mirrors `modes.ts`, held equal in English by `i18n.test.ts`).
- **Mirrors.** When a screen shows words the engine or bundled logic keeps (a tier, a formation's blurb, a power-up, a
  rules chip, a Run-in job), it calls a mirror in `src/ui` that reads the same words through `t()`, keyed by the
  engine's id (`i18n/engineText.ts`, `i18n/tierText.ts`, `i18n/weeklyThemeText.ts`, `slotRatingText.ts`,
  `runInText.ts`). `src/ui/__tests__/copyMirror.test.ts` holds each mirror equal to its source in English, so a change
  on either side fails until both agree; the one deliberate difference is a mode card's Hard with no re-spins, which
  reads "No re-spins" on screen.
- **Lines the logic writes (r10).** A sentence the engine or `src/game/logic` builds from facts (an Inbox item, a
  briefing line, a dressing-room reason, a morale reason, an Office answer or toast, a round name, the pundit's verdict,
  a setup summary) reads through `logicLine(text)` (`src/ui/i18n/logicText.ts`; `shellLogicLine` from `logicLine.ts` in
  App.tsx's digest and the toast, which must not pull the catalogue into the first download). The `logicText` catalogue
  holds the logic's English as ICU messages, `{holes}` where the facts go, plurals and selects where the English varies
  (`'{n, plural, one {# match} other {# matches}}'`); in another language a line is matched against them, the most
  specific first, and said again with what its holes held, each hole mirrored in turn; a list the logic joined with
  `, ` or ` · ` reads through `logicList(text, separator)`. English is returned untouched. A new line in the logic
  that reaches a screen gets its message there (`src/ui/__tests__/logicMirror.test.ts` plays a Season, the Office and a
  Dynasty summer and fails on a line that reads back differently, or on too many unmatched); a message too general to
  stand alone ("{objective}: {risk}") goes in `STRICT`, so it matches only when every hole is itself a known line. The
  online layer's own words (the save status, this device's posts, a failed read) are mirrored from the facts in
  `src/ui/online/statusText.ts`. Still in English: the match commentary and the match events' text (the live feed's
  whistle lines excepted), and the data credits in How it works.
- **Room to grow.** A translation runs about 30 % longer than English (German more). Buttons and chips wrap or
  ellipsise rather than overflow; never size a box to its English text. The development pseudo-locale shows it: in a
  dev build pick "Pseudo (en-XA)" under Profile, Settings, Language (or set `localStorage['touchline-language'] =
  'en-XA'` in any build) and every string through `t()` reads accented, bracketed and padded
  ("[!! Ŝéţûþ !!]"); plain English left on screen is a string that skipped `t()`.
- **Choosing a language.** Per viewer, `touchline-language` (never in the save): Auto (the default) follows
  `navigator.languages` to a language TouchLine has, else English. A translation is `src/ui/i18n/locales/<id>.ts`
  (`locales/README.md`), its own chunk loaded only when chosen; a key it leaves out reads in English. `<html lang>`
  follows the language on screen.
- **Tests.** `i18n.test.ts` (the helper, the catalogues, translations against the English) and
  `i18nScreens.test.ts` (every migrated screen server-rendered in English and in the pseudo-locale: no missing key,
  sound markup, and a per-screen ceiling on English left over that may only go down; `I18N_REPORT=1` lists it).
